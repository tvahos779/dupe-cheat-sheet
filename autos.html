INVOICE IS THEATER
The real math

What a $32,000 RAV4 actually costs the dealer

LineAmount
MSRP (sticker)$32,000
Invoice (what they show you)$29,800
Holdback — 3% of MSRP, paid back to the dealer after the sale−$960
Regional advertising credits−$1,200
Manufacturer-to-dealer incentive−$1,500
True dealer cost≈ $26,140

Targets: 1–3% over invoice = good deal. 8–12% off MSRP = ceiling, high-inventory or year-end only. A $28,500 sale still clears the dealer $2,360.

SOURCE · michaelryanmoney.com · engineerfix.com · eathealthy365.com — read Oct 24, 2026

Negotiate the out-the-door number. Only that number.

Before contact: pull invoice, regional average-paid, and current incentives (Edmunds / KBB / TrueCar).
Find the exact stock number and its days-on-lot on the dealer's own site. 90+ days = floorplan interest = motivated seller.
Email three dealers within 100 miles for written out-the-door quotes. Cascade the quotes against each other.
Settle the vehicle price first. Rebates come off after — they're the automaker's money, not the dealer's discount.
Never negotiate the monthly payment or the "MSRP discount." One number: OTD.
Walk away freely. The call-back is real — Tue–Thu, last week of the month, quota pressure peaks.

Salt-belt rust inspection — non-negotiable

Rust can cut 20–40% of a car's value; frame/suspension repairs run $2,000–5,000+ vs $300–500 for prevention. Clean body panels can hide structural rot. Get the car on a lift before money moves. No lift, no deal.

SOURCE · carbuzz.com · thegarageguru.net · thehealthyattitude.com — read Oct 24, 2026
  • On the lift: frame rails, subframe and suspension mounts, floor pans, rocker panels and pinch welds.
  • Brake and fuel lines: corroded lines are a safety hazard, not a cosmetic note — budget or walk.
  • Key test: tap suspect metal with a key. Dense thunk = solid. Hollow or papery = compromised.
  • Feel behind wheel-well liners for crunchy, flaky material; check hood/trunk seam welds, trunk lips, door jambs and seams.
  • History check: pull CARFAX/AutoCheck for where the car lived. A 5-year-old Vermont car can be rustier than a 15-year-old New Mexico car — prefer Southern / dry-state cars.
