The Scene

Weekly Chicago culture read · Oct 23, 2026

The Slide Is Real — Except When It Isn't

Every ticket in Chicago is falling in price right up until the moment it isn't. Knowing which show is which is the whole game.

The data says wait. Across thousands of resale purchases, day-of tickets average 33% below the average price and day-before 27% below, with the slide starting two to four weeks out as sellers get anxious. Festival passes bottom out around 13 days out, ~30% under. Multi-night runs slide on the later nights — John Summit's Wednesday at the United Center is sitting $15 under his Tuesday right now, same show, same room.

Then there's primetime. Bears–Patriots on Thursday night didn't slide — the open-market floor rose 23% in two days into kickoff, and Bears–Bucs on Sunday night is doing it again, up 24% in five days with 17 days still to go. The 2–4-week rule is a regular-show rule. Rivalry games, Christmas games (Bears–Packers, Dec 25 — start watching that one now), and anything with a kickoff time in the title play by different math: the crowd isn't getting anxious, it's getting bigger.

The third pattern is the legacy spread. Stevie Wonder plays six American dates this fall and Chicago is the cheapest seat in the country — $412 against $1,200+ at MSG. That's not a pricing error; it's demand geography, and it's repeatable. Cleo Sol's first arena tour routes MSG → Toronto → Chicago next April, and the middle-of-the-run Chicago date is exactly where that same softness tends to land. When a legend or a first-timer routes through here mid-run, Chicago is the value date. Buy like it.

How UFC tickets price

When to buy: numbered events — face value at on-sale is the floor for lower-bowl; upper-bowl resale often drifts down in the final week, except MSG and year-end Vegas cards, which hold their price. International Fight Nights sell at face through the local platform and can sell out — buy those early.

Where the fees hide: Ticketmaster and StubHub add service fees at the last checkout step — on a $300 seat the gap can top $60. TickPick and TicketIQ quote all-in, no-fee prices. Always compare the final total, never the list price.

The APEX exception: APEX Fight Nights aren't normal inventory — the room is mostly VIP and hospitality, public listings are thin, and get-ins start where arena uppers end. Treat $250–$400 as the going rate, and expect VIP packages, not discounts, as the upgrade path.
