Flights out of Chicago

The system first: alerts catch the anomaly, search frames the trip, cross-check proves it, book direct keeps it clean.

STEP 1AlertsGoing (ORD/MDW-filtered), Google Flights tracking, Secret Flying, FlyerTalk — where mistake fares surface first.
STEP 2SearchGoogle Flights date grid + Explore map. Skyscanner for the broadest market cross-check and "Everywhere" trips.
STEP 3VerifyRe-price on the airline's own site. Check the fare-class letter in Details before you believe the price.
STEP 4Book directSmooth refunds and changes, nobody between you and the airline when things break.

When to book — the 2026 math

  • Domestic sweet spot: 15–30 days out — averages ~$130 less than booking 180+ days ahead (Expedia's 2026 Air Hacks data). Google's five-year average low lands at 39 days.
  • Friday is the cheapest day to book (~3% under Sunday). "Book on Tuesday" is dead.
  • Tuesday is the cheapest day to fly domestically — 14% under Sunday, the most expensive day both ways.
  • January is the cheapest month to fly; Vegas, Orlando and Fort Lauderdale run ~25% below the national average fare.
  • Holidays: have tickets done by Halloween. Christmas books best ~56 days out, Thanksgiving ~35.
  • Hub attacks happen: a rival airline undercuts into ORD for a few days, unadvertised. Airfarewatchdog route alerts are the countermeasure.

ORD vs MDW — price both, every time

O'Hare (ORD)

United + American fortress hub and the international gateway. Best nonstops, and United-vs-American fare wars on overlapping routes. Blue Line downtown in 40–45 min, runs 24/7.

Midway (MDW)

Southwest's base (~90% of traffic), smaller and faster curb-to-gate, Orange Line in 25–30 min. Southwest's Tue/Wed flash sales live here — and the re-fare trick still works: no change fees, so rebook when the price drops and pocket the credit.

The 2026 catch: Southwest's free-bag era is over — first bag is $45 like everyone else. Run the bag math before assuming MDW wins; the old fee edge is gone.

⚡ Mistake fares — the 4-hour protocol

A real pricing error — a misfiled fare, a currency slip, a dropped zero. Airlines fix them within hours, so the playbook is speed plus discipline:

  • Book direct with the airline, inside the first 2–4 hours. OTA bookings can die silently if the carrier rejects the fare.
  • Capture the fare basis and the 13-digit ticket number — that's your proof.
  • Buy nothing non-refundable (hotels, activities) until the ticket confirms — 24–48 hours.
  • US carriers aren't legally forced to honor errors, but DOT rules require honor-or-full-refund — in practice, domestic mistake fares usually stick.

Fastest sources: FlyerTalk's Mileage Run forums (zero-day), Secret Flying's mistake-fare desk, Going Premium ($49/yr, vetted, filtered to ORD/MDW).

The Basic Economy trap

Fare type isn't fare class — and in 2026 Basic is a trap door:

  • No changes or cancellations on American, Delta or United Basic. One moved plan and "Basic" costs more than Main Cabin.
  • American Basic earns zero AAdvantage miles (since Dec 17, 2025). Delta Basic earns zero SkyMiles/Medallion credit.
  • United Basic restricts full-size carry-ons; American Basic boards Group 9, last.
  • Pro move: check the booking-code letter at checkout (Y = full-fare economy, N = United Basic, B = American Basic). Two "Main Cabin" results can be wildly different tickets.

Cancún & All-Inclusives

All-inclusive preferred — but "all-inclusive" varies by property, so every card reads the inclusions line by line.

✈ From Chicago: nonstop ORD→CUN (~3.5–4 hrs) · pre-book the airport transfer (Happy Shuttle / USA Transfers, ~$18pp shared) and walk straight past the shark tank — your driver waits outside · Visitax ~$15 and the ~$4–5/night environmental tax land at the edges · storm-season trips: insurance bought the day you book.

Costco Travel — the value anchor

For Mexico and the Caribbean, Costco is the benchmark every other package gets priced against — on total value, never the room rate alone:

  • Digital Shop Card lands by email after the trip — $50 to $500+ back, depending on the package.
  • Airport transfers included (typically shared; private on select packages — check the listing), plus hotel taxes and gratuities in the price.
  • Executive members stack 2% back (up to $1,250/yr) — but you must still be Executive when the trip begins or you forfeit it. The upgrade pays for itself at ~$3,250/yr of travel.
  • Airfare is never sold standalone — it bundles. Selection is curated and smaller than Expedia's; that's the trade.

The ALG trio — one owner, three storefronts

Apple Vacations, CheapCaribbean and Funjet are all ALG Vacations (Hyatt) — same inventory engine, different shop windows. Their "up to 75% off" is off brochure rates, not street.

  • Price the same package — same dates, room, departure airport — across all three, plus Kayak and Expedia. In 2026 price tests no single brand always won; Funjet and CheapCaribbean each took different packages.
  • Apple runs a published price-match policy (same-day, every component identical). Deposits run $250–350/person.
  • Airline vacation arms (Southwest / United / JetBlue Vacations) sometimes undercut — and you earn miles on the package.

Sargassum season runs ~April–August. A cheap July all-inclusive can mean a rotting-seaweed beach. The price is only a deal if the beach is swimmable — check the sargassum report before booking summer dates.

Do this, skip that — Riviera Maya

  • Snorkel Puerto Morelos, not the Hotel Zone — shore-entry reef, small boats booked at the pier for less than the hotel desk charges. Hotel Zone beaches have nothing worth seeing from shore.
  • Mornings only. Wind kills visibility after 11 AM. Akumal's turtle zone is guided-only; whale sharks run from June.
  • Xel-Há math: the all-inclusive ticket covers river, gear, lockers, bikes, tubes, buffet and domestic drinks — zip-lines and photo packages are extra. Know it before the upsell.

Field intel: Mr Playas (Reddit-sourced snorkel verdicts) and Discover with Orlando, a 10-year Riviera Maya tour operator — logistics gold, recommendations read as top-of-funnel sales.

Puerto Rico

The Caribbean run with no passport, no currency exchange, and a 500-year-old city at the end of the flight.

✈ From Chicago: nonstop ORD→SJU (~5 hrs) — no passport, dollars, US cell service · SJU to Condado or Old San Juan is ~15 min · anything west of the metro (Rincón stays, a Guavate Sunday) means a rental car, booked with the flight.

Why PR runs different

  • No passport, US dollars, US cell service — the easiest Caribbean trip there is from ORD, and a natural pairing with a San Juan cruise departure.
  • Base in Condado or Old San Juan: Condado for the beach-and-lagoon strip (Ashford Avenue, Ventana al Mar), Old San Juan for the port-day walk — Paseo de la Princesa, the Raíces fountain, the city-wall perimeter.
  • See it before you book it: TravelWithSilent's no-narration walking tours cover both neighborhoods end to end — the rare preview that shows you the street, not the sales pitch.

Cruises

Agencies win on price, direct wins on simplicity — and nothing about a price drop is automatic. You spot it, you file it.

✈ From Chicago: every sailing here leaves a fly-to port — Miami, Port Canaveral, Galveston or San Juan · fly in the day before, always, and add the buffer night and the flights to the true total · price ORD/MDW against MKE/RFD for the port flight before you book either.

Where cruise prices actually move

  • VacationsToGo's 90-Day Ticker is the last-minute clearinghouse — sailings inside 90 days, average availability under a week. No booking fees. Their real edge is group space: agency-blocked cabins at negotiated rates, forum-verified at roughly half the line's direct price on the same sailing. Always ask: assigned cabin, or GTY (cheaper, but the line picks your spot)?
  • CruiseCompete: submit one sailing, get 3–5 agency bids that must include all taxes and fees. Same-cabin gaps of 5–15% are common.
  • Quiet offers: published prices are retail. A phone quote unlocks unpublished reductions and commission rebated as onboard credit — $100–300 is routine. Never pay an agency booking fee for a cruise; the good ones don't charge one.
  • Repositioning cruises (Apr–May, Sep–Nov) run 50–70% cheaper per day — more sea days, oddball itineraries, real value if the sea days are the point.

Southwest & West Coast

One run, two landscapes — saguaro desert and Pacific coast, grouped because they pair in a single trip and price against each other.

✈ From Chicago: Tucson — nonstop ORD→TUS, or PHX + a ~2-hr desert drive; San Diego — nonstop ORD/MDW→SAN · both are hotel last-minute towns (the 8–14-day window), never flight last-minute towns · the pair also flies as one trip: into TUS, out of SAN.

🌵 Tucson — the desert run

Saguaros, a missile silo, and a UNESCO food city. The thinnest video lane of any destination here — so this file is built from written intel.

One visit? Go West

  • Saguaro West beats East for a first trip — the densest saguaro stands, the Bajada Loop Drive, Signal Hill petroglyphs. Best short hike: Valley View Overlook.
  • $25 per vehicle covers both districts for 7 days — the cheapest headline act on this whole sheet.
  • Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum — 98 acres of zoo, botanical garden and earth museum in one, with the Raptor Free Flight show.
  • Titan Missile Museum — the underground tour books out; reserve ahead. San Xavier del Bac, the "White Dove of the Desert," is the other essential stop.

Eat like it's designated

Tucson is a UNESCO City of Gastronomy — the Mexican food is the attraction, not a side quest. Work 4th Avenue's vintage strip between meals.

Honest gap, stated plainly: no standout Tucson YouTuber surfaced in the research — every other destination here has a voice; Tucson's intel is written-source. The full city file adds lodging math (desert resorts vs in-town value), TUS fare patterns, and the winter-season pricing curve.

🌊 San Diego — the coast run

70 and sunny, priced like it knows it. The play is hotel discipline and a tight 48 hours.

The 48-hour hit list

  • Balboa Park — Spanish Revival architecture, museums, gardens.
  • Point Loma & Cabrillo — the 1542 landing site, WWII bunkers, harbor views; Mount Soledad for the panorama.

Worth the flight: food destinations

The rest of the planet's table. The home-turf destinations eat in their own sections — these are the food trips worth a long flight: the dish that justifies the airfare, what eating well actually costs, when to go, how you get there, and the catch.

🍜 Tokyo — the yen is the discount

The dish

Ramen, in a country that treats a noodle bowl like a craft discipline — then the whole ladder behind it: conveyor-belt sushi, depachika basement food halls, standing sake bars. There is no single plate; the city itself is the dish.

Eating well costs

Less than it should — the yen sits near a four-decade low (Going's 2026 budget file), so the exchange rate does the discounting. No coupons, no happy hours: world-class eating at prices that read like a mid-tier US city.

When to go

Late Oct–Nov: fall color, comfortable walking weather between meals, and the new-route launch window on fares.

Getting there

Three nonstops by spring 2027: United flies ORD→Haneda and restores daily ORD→Narita from Oct 24, 2026; American enters the route Mar 27, 2027 — competition is the fare play.

The catch

The best rooms seat eight people and the queue is the reservation system. And it's ~13 hours in the air — build the trip around eating and the flight earns it.

🥩 Seoul — barbecue as a team sport

The dish

Korean barbecue — you grill it yourself, wrap it in lettuce with garlic and ssamjang, and the banchan keep coming free. Then Gwangjang Market for bindaetteok pancakes and knife-cut noodles, eaten shoulder to shoulder.

Eating well costs

Seoul keeps landing on Chicago's December deal boards, and the won stretches on the ground: market meals price like snacks, and a full barbecue spread for the table costs what one steakhouse entrée does at home.

When to go

December for the deal fares — the cold is exactly why they're deals. April–May trades the discount for blossom season.

Getting there

ORD→Incheon nonstop (Korean Air / Asiana), then the AREX express train puts you downtown in under an hour.

The catch

December averages −3°C at night to 4°C by day — this is a winter-city food trip, gloves between restaurants. The nonstop is 13–14 hours.

🍲 Hanoi & Hoi An — phở for pocket change

The dish

Phở at a Hanoi street stall at 7 AM — the real breakfast of the city — and in Hoi An, cao lầu, the noodle dish that exists only there, plus white-rose dumplings in the lantern-lit old town.

Eating well costs

Going's line says it best: a bowl of phở costs less than your morning coffee back home. A comfortable mid-range Vietnam trip — good hotels, sit-down dinners, domestic hops — runs $80–150 a day all-in.

When to go

February–April or August–October — the shoulder windows that dodge both the Tết (Lunar New Year) price surge and peak-season airfare.

Getting there

⚠ Connections — no nonstop exists from Chicago; routings run via an Asian hub into Hanoi (HAN) or Ho Chi Minh City (SGN), with cheap domestic flights stitching the country together.

The catch

The traffic is a contact sport and crossing the street is a learned skill. Two stops each way is the real price — the food is the rebate.

🌶 Bangkok & Chiang Mai — the street-food capital

The dish

Street food as fine dining: wok-fired pad see ew, boat noodles, mango sticky rice — Bangkok's stalls hold Michelin listings, and Chiang Mai adds khao soi, the curry-noodle bowl the north guards jealously.

Eating well costs

Backpacker math runs $25–50 a day (Going) and comfort doesn't cost much more. The islands — Phuket, Koh Samui — run 40–60% pricier than Chiang Mai; the value lives on the mainland and up north.

When to go

May–June or September–October shoulders price below the Nov–Mar high season; Nov–Feb is the cool, dry classic at higher rates.

Getting there

⚠ Connections — via Bangkok (BKK) on one stop from a hub, then a 1-hour hop or the overnight train to Chiang Mai. A new 300-baht (~$9) tourist entry fee applies on air arrivals from 2026.

The catch

Heat is the house condition, and Feb–Apr brings the north's smoky season — the shoulder windows above are picked to dodge it.

🥧 Lisbon — Europe's affordable table

The dish

Pastel de nata, warm and blistered, eaten standing at the counter with cinnamon — then tasca lunches: bifana pork sandwiches, grilled fish, the fixed-price prato do dia that feeds the neighborhood.

Eating well costs

Portugal's cost base runs roughly one-third lower than the US, and spring shoulder fares compress the flight on top of it. It's the full European food trip at a daily spend that feels domestic.

When to go

Spring shoulder (Mar–May) — mild weather, sardine season approaching, fares below summer. Early fall is the understudy.

Getting there

ORD→Lisbon nonstop (TAP) — one of the shortest Europe hops on this page, which makes it the easiest long weekend of the six.

The catch

The hills are real and the center's prices are creeping as the secret spreads. Eat a few neighborhoods out — that's where the tascas still price for locals.

🍢 Marrakech — dinner in the open-air food fair

The dish

Jemaa el-Fnaa at dusk — a square that turns into a hundred-stall open-air restaurant every night: grilled skewers, harira soup, snail broth for the brave, then a tagine lunch the next day inside the medina.

Eating well costs

Morocco sits in the $35–60 a day band on the 2026 cheapest-countries tables — riad breakfasts, market lunches and a full medina dinner for what a single entrée costs at home.

When to go

Spring and fall shoulders — summer is desert-hot and winter nights in the medina run cold. The food fair runs every night of the year.

Getting there

⚠ Connections — no ORD nonstop exists; routings run via European hubs or Casablanca. Riads are hidden in the medina maze — the good ones send someone to meet you at the square.

Last-minute mechanics — where late actually pays

  • Last-minute is a hotel and cruise game, not a flight game. Flights inside 14–21 days usually cost more — the cheap buckets close for business travelers. Exceptions: mistake fares, and Southwest flash sales plus the re-fare trick.
  • Hotels: the sweet spot is 8–14 days out (~20%+ cheaper); same-day runs ~14% cheaper than a week out. Channels: Priceline Express (40–60% off) and Hotwire Hot Rates (30–60%) — opaque, so you pay first and learn the name second, non-refundable. HotelTonight owns same-day; chain apps direct still price-match.
  • Cruises: the 60–90 day window after final-payment deadlines is the classic play — but 2026 runs an early-bird model, with the lowest prices 6–18 months out. Peak sailings (Christmas, new ships) never drop. They climb.
  • Packages: Apple Vacations' "Last Minute Deals" filter (60 days or less) and Mystery Deals (up to 75% off, resort revealed 2 days out); Costco Travel keeps a last-minute page too.
  • Never last-minute a must-be-there trip. Set a cutoff date — if the deal hasn't appeared by then, book normally and stop watching.

The May loophole: Caribbean all-inclusives hit their floor prices in May — the same floor as September/October — with zero storm risk. It's the single best risk-adjusted month on this whole sheet.

Christmas week (Dec 20–Jan 2) is the most expensive travel week of the year at every destination here. Any "deal" in that window that doesn't price in the holiday premium isn't one.

  • Cruises: book in wave season, Jan–Mar; sail cheap Sep–Oct, early Nov, Jan.
  • Vegas: the annual 5-Day Sale (late Sept) books Feb–Mar travel at up to 60% off.
  • Holidays: Christmas flights ~56 days out, Thanksgiving ~35 — tickets done by Halloween.
