Las Vegas

✈ From Chicago: nonstops ORD/MDW→LAS all day (~4 hrs) · the 15–30-day booking window is the sweet spot · the Strip is ~15 min from LAS · run every room through the fee calculator below before booking — and when a fare looks fat, MKE and RFD get priced too.

⚡ Tips & tricks — the short version

  • Price the fee, not the rate: a $0-fee room at $119 beats a $55-fee room at $89. The calculator below runs the real math.
  • The only true $0-fee rooms: Casino Royale (center Strip), Four Queens and Binion's (downtown). The Golden Nugget downtown still charges $46.
  • Parking is a fee too — $20–25/day on the Strip unless you're MGM Pearl+ or Caesars Platinum+.
  • Las Vegas Advisor's ~$50 coupon book routinely pays for itself in a single night — the highest-ROI Vegas buy there is.
  • “Free guest list” means you're the product. Skip it — pay only for a verified presale under the door price.

Cancún & All-Inclusives

✈ From Chicago: nonstop ORD→CUN (~3.5–4 hrs) · pre-book the airport transfer (Happy Shuttle / USA Transfers, ~$18pp shared) and walk straight past the shark tank — your driver waits outside · Visitax ~$15 and the ~$4–5/night environmental tax land at the edges · storm-season trips: insurance bought the day you book.

⚡ Tips & tricks — the short version

  • Pick your coast first. Hotel Zone: 15 min from the airport, the party strip, the priciest beds — the right call for 3-night runs. Riviera Maya: the value coast — bigger resorts, lower nightly rates, but a 45–90 min transfer and your excursions (cenotes, Tulum, Xel-Há) are suddenly close. Costa Mujeres: the newest builds on the clearest water, ~45 min north — quiet, and there's almost nothing off-resort. That's the point, or the problem.
  • Price Costco first — transfers, taxes and gratuities in, Shop Card back after the trip. Every other package has to beat that total.
  • Apple, CheapCaribbean and Funjet are one owner (ALG) — price the identical package on all three; no single storefront always wins.
  • Read the inclusions like a contract. "All-inclusive" is a spectrum: how many à la carte restaurants, and can you actually get in (the value tier makes you queue at 7 AM for reservations, or caps your visits)? Is top-shelf liquor in, or is that an upcharge tier? Room service, minibar, Wi-Fi — the cheap quote excludes at least one. The inclusions line decides the trip; the room photo doesn't.
  • Sargassum reality check: it runs ~Apr–Aug and peaks in summer — a cheap July package can mean a brown, smelly beach. North-facing water (Costa Mujeres, Isla Mujeres) dodges the worst of it. Check the beach report the morning you swim, and make sure the resort's pools can carry the day if the sea can't.
  • May is the loophole: floor prices with zero storm risk. September is cheaper — and it's the hurricane peak. Refundable only, insurance bought the day you book.
  • Pre-book the airport transfer (~$18pp) and walk straight past the timeshare shark tank — your driver waits outside.

Puerto Rico

✈ From Chicago: nonstop ORD→SJU (~5 hrs) — no passport, dollars, US cell service · SJU to Condado or Old San Juan is ~15 min · anything west of the metro (Rincón stays, a Guavate Sunday) means a rental car, booked with the flight.

⚡ Tips & tricks — the short version

  • No passport, US dollars, US cell service — the easiest Caribbean trip there is from ORD, ~5 hrs nonstop to SJU. And Chicago-only: no MKE or RFD nonstop exists (§01).
  • The cruise-pairing play: San Juan is a homeport, not a port of call — Rhapsody of the Seas, Norwegian Prima, Virgin's Valiant Lady, Crown & Emerald Princess, Celebrity Constellation & Summit sail 7-night Southern Caribbean loops from here (Aruba, Curaçao, Barbados, St. Lucia) that Florida ships can't reach in a week. Fly in the day before, sleep in Old San Juan, walk to the pier. The Southern Caribbean deep dive is in §06.
  • Condado: the beach-and-lagoon strip, 10 min from the piers — the pre/post-cruise base. Old San Juan: 500 years of city, the port at your feet — stay here for the night-before, not the beach week. Isla Verde: the best sand in the metro and the big resorts, 15 min from SJU.
  • The honest AI note: Puerto Rico has almost no true all-inclusives — the beach resorts price room + food à la carte. So price them line-for-line against the Cancún totals in §03 before you book. PR wins on the city, the cruise pairing and zero passport friction; Cancún wins the pure beach math. Know which trip you're buying.
  • Rentals beat hotels structurally: ~$206/night average vs $332 — check rentals before hotels.
  • Mid-Apr–Jun is the value window — 20–35% below winter.
  • Anything west (Rincón) means a rental car — it's ~2.5 hrs from San Juan. Book it with the flight.

Greece

✈ From Chicago: ORD→ATH nonstop on United and American, daily in season (~10h15 out, ~11h40 home) · Athens is the hub — the islands run on short domestic flights and ferries · September is the fare trough: ~$669 average round trip, and anything under ~$819 beats most of the year.

⚡ Tips & tricks — the short version

  • 7 days minimum, full stop — it's a full travel day each way, and a short hop never earns the airfare out here.
  • September is the play: the cheapest ORD→ATH fares of the year (~$669 average), the sea still warm, the crowds gone.
  • Shape it 3 nights Athens + 3–4 on one island. Two bases, max — daily ferry-hopping burns the week in terminals.
  • Book the fast ferries. The slow boats eat half a day each.

Cruises

✈ From Chicago: every sailing here leaves a fly-to port — Miami, Port Canaveral, Galveston or San Juan · fly in the day before, always, and add the buffer night and the flights to the true total · price ORD/MDW against MKE/RFD for the port flight before you book either.

⚡ Tips & tricks — the short version

  • Compare all-in per person, per day — fare + port taxes + gratuities + the add-on stack. The comparator below runs it line by line.
  • 72% of sailings dip 5%+ after booking. Reprice before final payment — nobody does it automatically; you spot it, you file it.
  • “Free” perks aren't: NCL's Free at Sea drink package carries $300–400+ per person in mandatory service charges.
  • Get 3–5 agency bids on the same cabin (CruiseCompete) — gaps of 5–15% are common, and never pay an agency booking fee.
  • Promo clock: Royal Caribbean's BOGO60 + kids-sail-free ends Nov 2, 2026. And fly in the day before. Always.

🌴 Southern Caribbean — the target trip

The ABC islands — Aruba, Bonaire, Curaçao — sit below the hurricane belt, with Barbados, St. Lucia, Antigua and St. Maarten ringing them east. Florida ships can't reach this water in a week. San Juan can. Fly in the day before, sleep in Old San Juan (§04), walk to the pier.

The routings — port by port

Three classic loops out of San Juan. The pattern is the point — exact port order shifts by sailing.

The ABC run

Day 1 San Juan, sail-away at night · Day 2 St. Kitts · Day 3 Martinique · Day 4 Aruba · Day 5 Curaçao · Day 6 🌊 sea day · Day 7 🌊 sea day north · Day 8 San Juan. The Virgin pattern — west for the ABCs, then two days to enjoy the ship you paid for.

The Windward run

Day 1 San Juan · Day 2 St. Thomas · Day 3 St. Croix · Day 4 St. Maarten · Day 5 Antigua · Day 6 Dominica · Day 7 🌊 sea day · Day 8 San Juan. The Rhapsody pattern — a port a day up the Windward chain, one breather before home.

The deep-south sampler

Day 1 San Juan · Day 2 Tortola · Day 3 St. Kitts · Day 4 Barbados · Day 5 St. Lucia · Day 6 Dominica · Day 7 🌊 sea day · Day 8 San Juan. The Celebrity pattern — the southernmost islands a 7-night loop can touch.

🏝 Aruba — the beach day

The move

Eagle Beach is the beach — wide, white, worth the short taxi (typical cab ~$15–20). Palm Beach, by the high-rises, is the loud version. The local play: rent a car south to Baby Beach — shallow, calm, zero scene.

Eat & drink

Zeerover, Savaneta — the day's catch, fried, eaten on the dock with the fishermen. Pastechi for breakfast; a Balashi, brewed on the island.

Timing

Ship crowds flood Palm Beach by 11; Eagle Beach absorbs them. Oranjestad traffic back to the pier is real — leave the sand an hour earlier than feels necessary.

🤿 Bonaire — the reef day

The move

The island is the excursion. Shore-diving capital of the world — the reef starts where the sand ends. Rent the truck-and-tank setup from any boulevard dive shop and pick your yellow rock markers; 1000 Steps and the Salt Pier are the greatest hits. No truck? The water taxi to Klein Bonaire is the best cheap snorkel in the Caribbean.

Eat & drink

Fresh fish at the harbor-front stalls in Kralendijk. Try the cactus soup once — you're here, it's good.

Timing

Everything is 15 minutes from the pier. Go early — reef light is best before noon and the cruise groups move slow.

🎨 Curaçao — the city day

The move

Walk off the ship. The Queen Emma floating bridge swings you into Punda, and the Handelskade — this photo — is the postcard, free. Beach: skip the port-side strip and taxi west to Playa Kenepa (typical cab ~$40–50 each way; split it four ways) — the clearest water on the island.

Eat & drink

Plaza Bieu, the old market hall — lunch counters doing stewed goat, iguana soup for the brave, and batidos. Then the original Blue Curaçao distillery at Landhuis Chobolobo.

Timing

Ships often sail late from Curaçao. Stay ashore for sunset — the bridge glows, the day-trippers are gone.

🐢 Barbados — the west-coast day

The move

West is the money coast — calm, clear, priced like it for a reason. Carlisle Bay, 10 minutes from the pier, runs turtle-and-wreck snorkel boats (typical trips ~$30–50). The cruise-line beach transfer sells you the same sand for more.

Eat & drink

A fish cutter at Cuz's stand by the bay — the island's great cheap sandwich. Mount Gay's distillery sits in Bridgetown: rum was invented here; act accordingly.

Timing

Carlisle Bay at 9 AM is turtles and empty water. By noon it's a parking lot of boats.

⛰ St. Lucia — the Pitons day

The move

The Pitons are the island; everything else is filler. Take the catamaran down the coast (typical full day ~$100–130 with lunch): sail past both peaks, snorkel at their feet, skip 90 minutes of switchbacks each way. The drive-through volcano is a drive-past — the Sulphur Springs mud baths are the version worth your hour.

Eat & drink

Green fig and saltfish — the national plate — at the Castries market. Bounty is the local rum.

Timing

The boats sell out. Book the catamaran before you sail, not at the pier — the road tours leave at 8 and still eat the day.

⚓ Antigua — the dockyard day

The move

"365 beaches" is the slogan; you need one. Dickenson Bay is the easy play (typical cab ~$15). The better day runs south: Nelson's Dockyard — a working Georgian naval base, not a replica — then the Shirley Heights lookout above it.

Eat & drink

Ducana (sweet-potato dumpling) with saltfish, and a rum punch wherever the grill smoke is thickest.

Timing

Dockyard in the cool morning, beach after lunch. Heritage Quay can wait — it's the same shops as every port.

✈️ St. Maarten — the two-countries day

The move

Two countries, one island — spend it on the French side. Grand Case eats better per capita than anywhere in the Caribbean; Marigot has the market and the fort view. Maho Beach (this photo) is a genuine spectacle for exactly one landing cycle. Then it stops being fun.

Eat & drink

The lolos — open-air grill shacks in Grand Case: ribs, johnny cakes, local prices. Guavaberry liqueur is the souvenir that isn't a magnet.

Timing

Great Bay beach sits by the pier — save it for the last hour. The island's traffic jams by 4; be back on the Dutch side early.

The San Juan playbook

  • Who sails it: Royal Caribbean's Rhapsody of the Seas (Vision-class, 1996 — the old hardware is the fare), Norwegian Prima, Virgin's Valiant Lady, Princess's Crown & Emerald, Celebrity's Constellation & Summit — 7-night loops all winter.
  • 7 nights vs longer: a week reaches Barbados and St. Lucia. The 10–11-night Summit and Constellation runs add the Grenadines and deeper ABC time — the extra days buy islands, not sea days.
  • Seasonality: the ABCs sit below the hurricane belt — a year-round bet. The Windward stops live in the Jun–Nov storm zone; winter sailings dodge the question entirely.
  • The pairing: nonstop in the day before (§04), one Old San Juan night, embark rested. After the cruise, decompress west — Rincón is carded in §04.

Reno + Lake Tahoe

✈ From Chicago: United nonstop ORD→RNO daily (~4 hrs 37 min) — the only nonstop on the route · RNO sits ~10 min from downtown · Tahoe runs ~45 min to the North Shore, ~55 to Stateline by rental car.

⚡ Tips & tricks — the short version

  • The nonstop is real: United flies ORD→RNO daily, and it's the only one — everything else connects and eats the weekend.
  • The fee is the rate. $25 rooms carry $42 fees; every card below prints the all-in math. Run it before you fall for a headline.
  • Downtown is one roof: THE ROW connects Eldorado, Silver Legacy and Circus Circus — three casinos, one fee structure, no going outside.
  • Tahoe is the day trip that becomes the trip. Rent the car for a day, not the week — Reno rooms cost a third of the lake's.
  • Winter is ski season: Mt. Rose is 25 minutes from downtown, the closest real skiing to any casino town in America. Summer is lake season. Shoulder months are when the rooms crater.

Punta Cana, Dominican Republic

✈ From Chicago: nonstop ORD→PUJ (~4.5 hrs) on United/American · Bávaro resorts sit ~25 min from the airport · pre-book the transfer and skip the taxi scrum · US dollars run the resort zone, pesos run everything else.

⚡ Tips & tricks — the short version

  • Price it against Cancún (§03) line by line. Punta Cana wins the same-tier math more often than not — and the beach is wider.
  • Know your coast: Bávaro is the main strip · Cap Cana is the gated luxury end · Uvero Alto is the quiet north · Macao is the surf beach, and the local one.
  • Priceline's Chicago bundles quietly include a rental car. You don't need one at an all-inclusive — but it's priced in either way, so the per-person numbers on the cards are honest totals.
  • PUJ runs on routing roulette: the package listings don't show the flight routing — pick the ORD→PUJ nonstop at booking, never the San Juan or Miami connection the algorithm offers first.
  • Sargassum hits this coast too (spring–summer, §03 has the full check). The big resorts rake daily; winter and early spring are the clean-water months.

Pacific Mexico — Cabo + Puerto Vallarta

✈ From Chicago: nonstops ORD→SJD (Cabo) and ORD→PVR (Vallarta/Nayarit), ~4.5 hrs each · Cabo's corridor runs 20–40 min from SJD · the Nayarit hotels sit 15–45 min north of PVR — one airport, two vibes.

⚡ Tips & tricks — the short version

  • The hedge logic: when Cancún's sargassum peaks or a storm spins up in the Caribbean, the Pacific side is usually clear. Different ocean, different problems.
  • Cabo's catch is the water. Most beaches are look-don't-swim — El Médano is the swimmable exception, and every Cabo card flags which side of that line its resort sits on.
  • Whale season runs Dec–Apr in Cabo and the Bay of Banderas — the one season where the Pacific beats the Caribbean outright.
  • PVR is two trips in one airport: Puerto Vallarta's malecón and old town to the south, the gated Nayarit resort strip to the north. Pick before you book the hotel.
  • Sunset is the Pacific's party trick — every resort here faces it. Cancún's face the sunrise. Plan the dinners accordingly.

New Orleans

✈ From Chicago: Southwest nonstop MDW→MSY (~2 hrs 15 min), United/American from ORD · MSY to the Quarter is ~25 min · the Canal St streetcar covers the rest — no rental car, ever.

⚡ Tips & tricks — the short version

  • Don't bring a car. Parking runs $45–60/night, the Quarter is hostile to driving, and every card that saves you from it says so.
  • The fee game is weaker here — several cards carry no resort fee or a waived one. The room tax (~15%+) is not weak. Know which of the two a rate is hiding.
  • Festival weeks double every rate in town — Mardi Gras, Jazz Fest. The weeks between festivals are the steals: same city, half the room.
  • December is the locals' secret: Réveillon menus, floor-level hotel rates, the city lit up. January, between conventions, runs second.
  • Eat one meal a day where there's no line. The Quarter is for dinner; the Garden District and the Bywater are for lunch.

Last-minute mechanics — where late actually pays

  • Last-minute is a hotel and cruise game, not a flight game. Flights inside 14–21 days usually cost more — the cheap buckets close for business travelers. The exception: Southwest flash sales plus the re-fare trick.
  • Hotels: the sweet spot is 8–14 days out (~20%+ cheaper); same-day runs ~14% cheaper than a week out. Channels: Priceline Express (40–60% off) and Hotwire Hot Rates (30–60%) — opaque, so you pay first and learn the name second, non-refundable. HotelTonight owns same-day; chain apps direct still price-match.
  • Cruises: the 60–90 day window after final-payment deadlines is the classic play — but 2026 runs an early-bird model, with the lowest prices 6–18 months out. Peak sailings (Christmas, new ships) never drop. They climb.
  • Packages: Apple Vacations' "Last Minute Deals" filter (60 days or less) and Mystery Deals (up to 75% off, resort revealed 2 days out); Costco Travel keeps a last-minute page too.
  • Never last-minute a must-be-there trip. Set a cutoff date — if the deal hasn't appeared by then, book normally and stop watching.

Christmas week (Dec 20–Jan 2) is the most expensive travel week of the year at every destination here. Any "deal" in that window that doesn't price in the holiday premium isn't one.

Book-by windows that matter

  • Cruises: book in wave season, Jan–Mar; sail cheap Sep–Oct, early Nov, Jan.
  • Vegas: the annual 5-Day Sale (late Sept) books Feb–Mar travel at up to 60% off.
  • Holidays: Christmas flights ~56 days out, Thanksgiving ~35 — tickets done by Halloween.

Worth the flight: food destinations

🍜 Tokyo — the yen is the discount

The dish

Ramen, in a country that treats a noodle bowl like a craft discipline — then the whole ladder behind it: conveyor-belt sushi, depachika basement food halls, standing sake bars. There is no single plate; the city itself is the dish.

Eating well costs

Less than it should — the yen sits near a four-decade low (Going's 2026 budget file), so the exchange rate does the discounting. No coupons, no happy hours: world-class eating at prices that read like a mid-tier US city.

When to go

Late Oct–Nov: fall color, comfortable walking weather between meals, and the new-route launch window on fares.

Getting there

Three nonstops by spring 2027: United flies ORD→Haneda and restores daily ORD→Narita from Oct 24, 2026; American enters the route Mar 27, 2027 — competition is the fare play.

The catch

The best rooms seat eight people and the queue is the reservation system. And it's ~13 hours in the air — build the trip around eating and the flight earns it.

🥩 Seoul — barbecue as a team sport

The dish

Korean barbecue — you grill it yourself, wrap it in lettuce with garlic and ssamjang, and the banchan keep coming free. Then Gwangjang Market for bindaetteok pancakes and knife-cut noodles, eaten shoulder to shoulder.

Eating well costs

Seoul keeps landing on Chicago's December deal boards, and the won stretches on the ground: market meals price like snacks, and a full barbecue spread for the table costs what one steakhouse entrée does at home.

When to go

December for the deal fares — the cold is exactly why they're deals. April–May trades the discount for blossom season.

Getting there

ORD→Incheon nonstop (Korean Air / Asiana), then the AREX express train puts you downtown in under an hour.

The catch

December averages −3°C at night to 4°C by day — this is a winter-city food trip, gloves between restaurants. The nonstop is 13–14 hours.

🍲 Hanoi & Hoi An — phở for pocket change

The dish

Phở at a Hanoi street stall at 7 AM — the real breakfast of the city — and in Hoi An, cao lầu, the noodle dish that exists only there, plus white-rose dumplings in the lantern-lit old town.

Eating well costs

Going's line says it best: a bowl of phở costs less than your morning coffee back home. A comfortable mid-range Vietnam trip — good hotels, sit-down dinners, domestic hops — runs $80–150 a day all-in.

When to go

February–April or August–October — the shoulder windows that dodge both the Tết (Lunar New Year) price surge and peak-season airfare.

Getting there

⚠ Connections — no nonstop exists from Chicago; routings run via an Asian hub into Hanoi (HAN) or Ho Chi Minh City (SGN), with cheap domestic flights stitching the country together.

The catch

The traffic is a contact sport and crossing the street is a learned skill. Two stops each way is the real price — the food is the rebate.

🌶 Bangkok & Chiang Mai — the street-food capital

The dish

Street food as fine dining: wok-fired pad see ew, boat noodles, mango sticky rice — Bangkok's stalls hold Michelin listings, and Chiang Mai adds khao soi, the curry-noodle bowl the north guards jealously.

Eating well costs

Backpacker math runs $25–50 a day (Going) and comfort doesn't cost much more. The islands — Phuket, Koh Samui — run 40–60% pricier than Chiang Mai; the value lives on the mainland and up north.

When to go

May–June or September–October shoulders price below the Nov–Mar high season; Nov–Feb is the cool, dry classic at higher rates.

Getting there

⚠ Connections — via Bangkok (BKK) on one stop from a hub, then a 1-hour hop or the overnight train to Chiang Mai. A new 300-baht (~$9) tourist entry fee applies on air arrivals from 2026.

The catch

Heat is the house condition, and Feb–Apr brings the north's smoky season — the shoulder windows above are picked to dodge it.

🥧 Lisbon — Europe's affordable table

The dish

Pastel de nata, warm and blistered, eaten standing at the counter with cinnamon — then tasca lunches: bifana pork sandwiches, grilled fish, the fixed-price prato do dia that feeds the neighborhood.

Eating well costs

Portugal's cost base runs roughly one-third lower than the US, and spring shoulder fares compress the flight on top of it. It's the full European food trip at a daily spend that feels domestic.

When to go

Spring shoulder (Mar–May) — mild weather, sardine season approaching, fares below summer. Early fall is the understudy.

Getting there

ORD→Lisbon nonstop (TAP) — one of the shortest Europe hops on this page, which makes it the easiest long weekend of the six.

The catch

The hills are real and the center's prices are creeping as the secret spreads. Eat a few neighborhoods out — that's where the tascas still price for locals.

🍢 Marrakech — dinner in the open-air food fair

The dish

Jemaa el-Fnaa at dusk — a square that turns into a hundred-stall open-air restaurant every night: grilled skewers, harira soup, snail broth for the brave, then a tagine lunch the next day inside the medina.

Eating well costs

Morocco sits in the $35–60 a day band on the 2026 cheapest-countries tables — riad breakfasts, market lunches and a full medina dinner for what a single entrée costs at home.

When to go

Spring and fall shoulders — summer is desert-hot and winter nights in the medina run cold. The food fair runs every night of the year.

Getting there

⚠ Connections — no ORD nonstop exists; routings run via European hubs or Casablanca. Riads are hidden in the medina maze — the good ones send someone to meet you at the square.

The catch

Haggling is the pricing system, not a scam — but agree every price before the service, from the taxi to the henna, and the trip stays fun.

Drive-To Weekend Escapes

🚗 From Crystal Lake: New Buffalo ~2 hrs · Galena ~3 hrs · Door County ~4 hrs.

🌅 Southwest Michigan — the sunset coast

Why

New Buffalo, St. Joseph, South Haven: dune beaches, the winery trail, Chicago's beach-house belt. Summer Saturdays price like a fly-to resort — the lake doesn't care what month it is. The landlords do.

When it drops

The pattern: after Labor Day, lakefront rentals step down in stages. September is the sweet spot — warmest lake water of the year, crowds gone. Deepest drops run Nov–Apr.

Logistics

~2 hrs on I-90/I-94. Amtrak stops in New Buffalo — the one escape that works without a car. Warren Dunes for the big sand, St. Joe for the pier.

🏛 Galena, IL — Main Street, 1855

Why

A lead-boom town frozen mid-boom: brick storefronts, Civil War mansions turned B&Bs, the DeSoto House — Illinois' oldest operating hotel. Summer and fall weekends sell it out at full freight.

When it drops

The pattern: midweek beats weekend all year. Jan–Mar — post-holiday, pre-spring — is when the inns run their softest rates. Fall-color weekends are peak; the town is identical in February.

Logistics

~3 hrs west on US-20. Park once — Main Street is the whole trip, and the DeSoto House has the garage. The hills are real; bring shoes.

🚗 Door County, WI — the peninsula

Why

300 miles of shoreline, five state parks, lighthouses and fish boils. In July it prices like the Hamptons. It isn't July forever.

When it drops

The pattern: rates step down after Labor Day, drop again when fall color fades (late Oct), and bottom out in the pre–Memorial Day spring. Half the peninsula closes mid-winter — shoulder is the play, January is a ghost town.

Logistics

~4 hrs via I-43. Bay side (Fish Creek, Ephraim) for sunsets; the lake side is quieter and cheaper. Add the Washington Island ferry if you want one more island.
