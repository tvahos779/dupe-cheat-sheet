THE FEATURE

The City That Goes Out

Chicago’s night life is a grand tour — a Moorish ballroom in Uptown, a low-ceilinged club room off Clark Street, a marquee burning over State — and it still sets the pace for every smaller room within driving distance.

Start at the Aragon, a ballroom built to look like a Spanish courtyard, where the ceiling pretends to be sky and a weeknight crowd files in like it’s a coronation. Cross town to the Metro, close enough to Wrigley Field to feel the neighborhood change mood with the season — sticky floors, low ceilings, bands playing like the room is a rumor they have to live up to. That’s Chicago’s trick: the rooms have strata. The Green Mill keeps jazz hours in Uptown like Prohibition never ended. Pilsen’s halls run a bass the old plaster barely tolerates. A marquee on State Street turns a school night into a premiere. And the crowd knows the liturgy by heart — arrive early, stay for the encore, argue the setlist on the platform while the last train decides whether the night is really over.

The smart regular doesn’t always ride downtown, though. McHenry County keeps its own covenant: a thousand-cap rock room in McHenry where the floor sings the chorus back, a vaudeville palace in Crystal Lake, an opera house holding its hush since 1889 — smaller rooms, same religion, home by midnight if you want to be. And when the city gets too pleased with itself, there’s Milwaukee: an easy run up the highway to the Pabst’s old-world hush and Brady Street still warming up, a whole scene running one notch less discovered, as if it agreed to keep the secret a little longer. The region’s night life isn’t a ladder with Chicago on top. It’s a circuit — and the regulars are the ones who know which leg to run.
